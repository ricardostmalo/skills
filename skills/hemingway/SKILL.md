# Hemingway

Say what happened. Use the nouns for the things and the verbs for the actions. Leave out what the reader can supply. The feeling is under the surface; if you name it, it dies.

## Structure

- Scenes in sequence. What was done, what was said, what was seen.
- Paragraphs of action are short. Paragraphs of dialogue are one line each.
- Time moves forward. No flashbacks unless a character remembers out loud.
- Ends on an action or an object, never on a reflection.

## Sentence habits

- Short sentences. Then a long one joined by "and" that keeps going through several actions without stopping.
- Concrete nouns: the river, the bottle, the rain, the bridge. Not "the environment."
- Verbs of doing: walked, drank, said, looked. Rarely "felt" or "thought."
- Almost no adverbs. "Said" for every line of dialogue, not "exclaimed" or "murmured."
- Repetition of a word rather than a synonym: "It was hot and the road was hot and the dust was hot."
- No semicolons. Few commas.

## Moves he makes

- Shows the emotion through what the character does with their hands.
- Lets dialogue carry the conflict, with what is not said mattering more.
- Describes a place with three physical details and stops.
- Omits the most important thing and lets the reader feel its absence (the iceberg).
- Uses "and" to link actions so they feel continuous and unreflective.

## Never

- No abstractions: love, courage, meaning, truth. Show the thing that has them.
- No adjectives of judgment: beautiful, terrible, wonderful.
- No explaining what a scene meant.
- No long words where the Anglo-Saxon one exists.

## Example

Before: "She felt an overwhelming sense of loss as she stood in the empty, echoing apartment where they had shared so many memories."

After: "She stood in the apartment. The chairs were gone and the marks were on the floor where the chairs had been. She went to the window and looked out and then she put on her coat."

## Check

Read each sentence. If it tells the reader what to feel, cut it and show what happened instead. Count the adjectives; halve them.
