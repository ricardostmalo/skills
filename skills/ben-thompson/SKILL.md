# Ben Thompson

An analyst with a fixed set of named frameworks, applied to the news of the week. The value is in the structure: every piece answers what happened, why, and what it means, in that order, with the same tools each time.

## Structure

- Opens by summarizing the news in one paragraph, usually with a quote from the announcement.
- Background section that recaps his prior analysis of this company with links.
- Applies a named framework (Aggregation Theory, the Smiling Curve, integration vs. modularity) explicitly.
- Sections with plain headings: "The Business Model," "The Competitive Landscape," "What This Means for X."
- Ends with implications, often for a company not mentioned in the news.

## Sentence habits

- Long, orderly sentences with clear antecedents.
- Frequent self-reference: "As I wrote in 2019..."
- Defined terms capitalized and used consistently.
- Block quotes from sources, followed by "This is exactly right" or "This misses the point."
- Measured tone; strong claims stated as analysis, not opinion.
- Charts and diagrams described in words when present.

## Moves he makes

- Explains a decision by the company's business model rather than its stated reasons.
- Contrasts two companies that look similar and shows they are structurally opposite.
- Predicts the next move by reasoning from incentives.
- Revisits a past prediction and grades it.
- Names who wins and who loses from a change.

## Never

- No hot takes. The analysis is the same on a slow week.
- No unexplained jargon; every framework is restated when used.
- No personal anecdote as evidence.
- No hedging to avoid being wrong; he grades himself later.

## Example

Before: "Apple announced a new subscription service, which could be significant."

After: "Apple's new service is not really about the service. Apple's business model is selling hardware at a premium, and services exist to raise the switching cost of leaving the hardware. Read the announcement through that lens and every odd detail, from the bundle pricing to the exclusivity terms, makes sense. It also explains why Google will not copy it, and why Spotify should be worried."

## Check

Did the piece answer why, using structure and incentives, rather than what the company said? If it only reported, it is not analysis yet.
