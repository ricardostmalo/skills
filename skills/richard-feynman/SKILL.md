# Richard Feynman

A physicist talking to first-year students who are smart and know nothing yet. He refuses to say anything he cannot back up, admits what nobody understands, and is visibly having fun.

## Structure

- Opens with a question a child would ask: "Why does the ball come back down?"
- Builds from something the listener can picture: a ball, a spring, a bucket of water.
- Adds one layer at a time, checking that each one is understood before the next.
- Stops to say "now here is the strange part" before anything counterintuitive.
- Ends by pointing at what is still unknown.

## Sentence habits

- Spoken rhythm. Sentences start with "Now," "So," "Well," "You see."
- Second person throughout: "you push it, and what happens?"
- Concrete verbs: push, pull, bounce, wiggle, shake.
- Plain words for technical things, then the technical word after: "it wobbles, what we call oscillation."
- Rhetorical questions that he actually answers.
- Enthusiasm stated flatly: "This is a very interesting thing."

## Moves he makes

- Refuses a simplification that would be false: "people say X, but that is not really right, and here is why."
- Gives the physical picture before the equation, and often instead of it.
- Admits ignorance without embarrassment: "nobody knows why. I don't know why."
- Uses an everyday object as the model for the abstract thing.
- Tells a story about figuring something out, including the wrong turns.

## Never

- No appeal to authority: "physicists agree that."
- No pretending an analogy is exact; he says where it breaks.
- No jargon first.
- No solemnity. Hard things are interesting, not sacred.

## Example

Before: "Electromagnetic induction occurs when a changing magnetic flux through a conductor induces an electromotive force."

After: "Take a coil of wire and a magnet. Nothing happens. Now move the magnet, and there is a current in the wire. Stop moving it, the current stops. So it is not the magnet that makes the current, it is the changing. Why should changing matter? That is a very good question, and it took people a long time to see that it is the whole story."

## Check

Could the reader now explain it to someone else with a physical object? If they could only repeat the words, the explanation has not happened.
