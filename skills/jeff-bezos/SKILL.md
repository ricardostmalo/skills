# Jeff Bezos

A founder writing to owners. Few ideas, held for decades, each explained with one example from the year. Plain language, no adjectives, a lot of "we."

## Structure

- Opens with a result or a principle, not a greeting.
- Short sections, each one conviction: customer obsession, long-term thinking, invention, high standards.
- Each conviction gets a specific example with a number from the year.
- Frequent callbacks to the first letter: "It's still Day 1."
- Ends by reattaching the original 1997 letter or restating the long-term view.

## Sentence habits

- Short to medium sentences. Declarative.
- "We" for the company, "I" rarely and only for personal conviction.
- Numbers stated plainly: "over 100 million," "a 20% reduction."
- Occasional coined phrase used consistently across years: "two-way doors," "disagree and commit."
- No adjectives that a reader could dispute. "Big" only if there is a number.
- Sentences that define a term and then use it.

## Moves he makes

- States a principle, then a mechanism, then an example, in three sentences.
- Distinguishes decisions by reversibility and assigns process accordingly.
- Talks about failure as the cost of invention, with specific failed products named.
- Explains a counterintuitive choice (low prices, free shipping) by the long-term math.
- Tells one story about a customer or an employee, briefly.

## Never

- No hype about the future. The long-term view is stated as a discipline, not a dream.
- No corporate abstractions: "synergy," "stakeholder value," "transformation."
- No apology tone. Setbacks are reported flatly.
- No triads for rhythm.

## Example

Before: "We remain committed to delivering value to customers through continuous innovation and operational excellence."

After: "Customers are always beautifully, wonderfully dissatisfied, even when they say they are happy. That is why we lowered prices 40 times this year. Nobody asked us to. We did it because a customer who is happy today will expect more tomorrow, and we would rather be the ones who deliver it."

## Check

Every principle has a number or a named example from this period. If one does not, either find one or cut the principle.
