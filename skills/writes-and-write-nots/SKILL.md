# Writes and write-nots

Graham's prediction: within a few decades most people will not be able to write, because AI removes the pressure that made them learn. Writing was always hard because thinking clearly is hard, and most people only did it because a job or a school demanded it. Take away the demand and the middle disappears. What is left is people who can write and people who cannot, with nobody in between.

The part that matters for this skill is why that is a loss. Writing is not a way to record thinking that already happened. It is how a certain kind of thinking happens at all. Leslie Lamport's line, which Graham quotes: if you are thinking without writing, you only think you are thinking. The people who stop writing will not just lose a skill. They will lose the thoughts they would have had while writing.

His analogy is physical strength after industrialization. Nobody needs to be strong anymore, so only people who choose to exercise are. Thinking is about to become the same kind of choice.

## The decision this skill makes

When a piece of writing comes up, ask which of two things it is:

**Output.** The thinking is done and the text just has to exist. A changelog, a status update, a summary of a meeting you attended, a cover letter for a job you already understand. Delegate it. Nothing is lost.

**Thinking.** You do not yet know what you believe, and writing is how you will find out. A strategy memo, a post-mortem, an essay, a hard email, a plan for the next year, an answer to "what do you want." If you delegate this, you get a document and skip the thought. The document will read fine and you will not know what you think.

For the second kind: write it yourself, badly, first. Then use the model to edit, tighten, or argue with you. The order matters. A model editing your rough draft sharpens your thinking. A model drafting from your bullet points replaces it.

## Procedure

1. Before delegating any writing, name which kind it is. If you cannot tell, it is the second kind.
2. For thinking pieces, produce a rough draft yourself. Spoken into a phone, typed in a text box, written in the wrong language. Length and quality do not matter. What matters is that the claims came out of your head in order.
3. Only then bring in the model: to cut, to find the weak claim, to ask what you meant in paragraph three.
4. Keep the rough draft. It is the record of what you actually thought before it was polished.

## Signs you delegated something you should have written

- You cannot summarize the document without rereading it.
- Someone asks a follow-up and you have to check what the document said.
- The document has a position you are not sure you hold.
- It reads better than anything you have ever written, and that is the problem.

## How this fits the rest of this repo

The voice skills here are for step 3, not step 2. They make a draft read like a specific person. They cannot make it think like one. If the draft did not start in a human head, the voice is a costume.
