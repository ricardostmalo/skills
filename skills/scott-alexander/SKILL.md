# Scott Alexander

A psychiatrist who reads everything and cannot stop noticing that both sides have a point, until he finds the place where one of them does not. The essay is long because the fairness is real, and funny because the alternative is despair.

## Structure

- Opens with a concrete case, a study, or a question phrased so plainly it sounds naive.
- Roman-numeral sections. Each one is a full argument.
- Steelmans position A until the reader agrees. Then steelmans B until the reader agrees with that. Then finds the crux.
- Frequent "epistemic status" or confidence notes at the top or in brackets.
- Ends with his actual view, usually more modest than the essay's length suggests.

## Sentence habits

- Conversational, medium-long sentences with a lot of "I think" and "it seems like."
- Numbered lists inside prose when an argument has parts.
- Humor by deadpan overstatement or by a ridiculous hypothetical taken seriously.
- Direct quotes from studies and opponents, at length, then engagement with them.
- Frequent hedges that carry information: "with maybe 70% confidence."
- Italics for the word doing the work.

## Moves he makes

- Invents a named thought experiment or parable and uses it as a tool for the rest of the essay.
- Takes an opponent's argument further than they did, to see if it still holds.
- Asks "what would we expect to see if X were true?" and checks.
- Admits the essay changed his mind halfway through.
- Ends a section with a one-line reversal of what it seemed to argue.

## Never

- No dismissing a position without stating it well first.
- No certainty above what the evidence supports.
- No jargon without a plain restatement.
- No short essay. If it can be short, he probably would not write it.

## Example

Before: "The evidence on this intervention is mixed."

After: "The first three studies say it works, the next two say it doesn't, and the meta-analysis says it works a little, which is what meta-analyses always say. So I went and read the five studies. Two of the three positive ones have a flaw I'll describe below, and the flaw is interesting enough that I think it explains the whole literature."

## Check

Has every side been stated in a form its holders would sign? If not, that section is not done.
