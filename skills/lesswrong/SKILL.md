# LessWrong

The forum voice, distinct from its two famous ends (Eliezer, dense and combative; Scott, long and funny). The center is earnest, structured, and calibrated: every claim carries a confidence, every disagreement is a crux to find, and the post exists to be updated by comments.

## Structure

- **Epistemic status** line at the top: "confident," "exploratory," "I've thought about this for a week, not a year."
- TL;DR or summary before the body.
- Numbered sections. Long posts get a table of contents.
- A "cruxes" or "what would change my mind" section near the end.
- Explicit acknowledgment of the strongest counterargument.
- Ends with a request for pushback or a list of open questions.

## Sentence habits

- Medium-long sentences with parenthetical qualifications.
- Probabilities in text: "I'd put this at 60%," "I updated from 20% to 35% after reading X."
- Community vocabulary used precisely: prior, update, steelman, crux, inside view / outside view, Goodhart, Chesterton's fence, motte-and-bailey, Moloch, alignment, capabilities.
- "I think," "it seems to me," "my model is" as honest markers, not padding.
- Links to prior posts as evidence of the concept, not just the term.
- Bold for defined terms on first use.

## Moves it makes

- Names the reasoning tool being used: "applying the outside view here..."
- Separates "what I believe" from "what I'd bet on" from "what I can argue for."
- Steelmans before disagreeing, and says so.
- Flags its own likely biases: "I notice I want this to be true, so discount accordingly."
- Proposes a concrete test or experiment that would resolve the disagreement.
- Thanks the commenter who changed its mind, in an edit at the top.

## Never

- No argument from authority. Credentials are mentioned only as a bias to correct for.
- No attacks on the person. Wrong ideas, not bad people.
- No unmarked certainty. A claim with no confidence attached reads as sloppy.
- No rhetoric that would work on someone who wasn't reasoning.
- No refusing to engage with an uncomfortable conclusion.

## Example

Before: "AI progress is going to be much faster than most people expect."

After: "Epistemic status: moderately confident in the direction, low confidence in the timeline. I think most people's model of AI progress is anchored on the last visible product rather than on the trend in capabilities per dollar, and that this anchoring predictably makes them too slow. My inside view says 2-4 years to X; the outside view (technologies usually take longer than insiders think) pulls me back to 4-8. I'd put 65% on 'faster than the median forecaster expects.' The crux for me is whether Y; if you have evidence on Y, that's where I'd update."

## Check

Does every claim carry a confidence? Is there a crux? Would the post's author be glad to be proven wrong in the comments? If any answer is no, it is not done.
