# Corporate

The register where nothing is said that could be quoted against the speaker. Every noun is abstract, every verb is "drive" or "leverage," every list has three items, and the passive voice ensures nobody did anything.

This skill exists mostly so you can see it. Everything in the "Moves" section is a thing to hunt in your own drafts.

## Vocabulary

- Verbs: leverage, drive, enable, align, optimize, streamline, unlock, empower, operationalize, socialize, action (as a verb), circle back, double-click, take offline.
- Nouns: synergies, stakeholders, value creation, key learnings, best practices, core competencies, bandwidth, alignment, visibility, deliverables, workstreams, low-hanging fruit, north star.
- Adjectives: robust, scalable, holistic, strategic, seamless, best-in-class, mission-critical, world-class, agile.
- Frames: "at the end of the day," "going forward," "net-net," "from a X perspective," "in terms of."

## Sub-dialects

**Banking.** "We remain constructive on the name." Numbers to two decimals, conclusions to none. "Attractive risk-reward." Everything is "well-positioned." Downside is "headwinds," upside is "tailwinds." The pitchbook triad: overview, opportunity, next steps.

**Consulting.** Everything is a framework with a two-by-two. "Our hypothesis is." "Key takeaways." Three bullets per slide, each with a bolded lead-in. Recommendations "phased" into "waves." Problems are "opportunities." The client's problem is always a lack of "alignment."

**Sales.** "Excited to." "Quick question." "Just following up." "Circling back." "I'd love to get 15 minutes on your calendar." Every feature "unlocks" something. Every customer "partners with" us. The competitor is never named and is always "legacy."

## Structure

- Opens with context nobody needed: "As you know, in today's rapidly evolving landscape..."
- Three sections. Always three.
- Bullet points with bolded lead-ins and a colon.
- Closes with "next steps" and an offer to "sync."

## Sentence habits

- Passive voice: "it was decided," "mistakes were made," "the decision was taken."
- Nominalizations: "the implementation of" instead of "we built."
- Hedges stacked: "may potentially," "could arguably."
- Triads everywhere: "people, process, and technology."
- Nothing concrete. No names, no numbers that could be checked, no dates that could be missed.

## Moves (hunt these in real writing)

- The passive that hides the actor.
- The abstraction that covers a concrete thing nobody would defend if named.
- The triad added for rhythm.
- The "going forward" that means "we are not fixing the past."
- The "excited to" that means nothing.
- The framework that restates the question as an answer.

## Example

Plain: "We lost the client because we shipped the report three weeks late."

Corporate: "Following a comprehensive review of the engagement, it was determined that certain timeline-related challenges impacted stakeholder alignment, resulting in a strategic decision by the client to explore alternative partnership models going forward. Key learnings have been captured and will be socialized across relevant workstreams."

## Check

Can you tell who did what? Could any sentence be quoted in a lawsuit? If the answers are "no" and "no," the register is correct. Now go rewrite it in the plain one.
